Derivation Analysis

Let $I = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-x^2} dx$. Multiply by the identical integral over $y$: $I^2 = \iint_{\mathbb{R}^2} e^{-(x^2+y^2)} dx\,dy$.

Converting to polar coordinates $(r, \theta)$: $I^2 = \int_0^{2\pi} d\theta \int_0^\infty e^{-r^2} r \, dr = 2\pi \cdot \left[ -\frac{1}{2} e^{-r^2} \right]_0^\infty = 2\pi \cdot \frac{1}{2} = \pi$. Thus, $I = \sqrt{\pi}$.
